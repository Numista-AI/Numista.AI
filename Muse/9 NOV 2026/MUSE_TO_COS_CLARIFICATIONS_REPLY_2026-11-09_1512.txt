ASK 1 — COMPLETION-PAGE DESIGN, RE-EVALUATED WITH fbclid FORWARDED
------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) IS IT A VALID STANDARD EVENT?  Yes. [SOURCED]
    CompleteRegistration is a predefined Meta standard event, defined as
    "submitting information in exchange for a service provided by your
    business. For example, signing up for an email subscription." The spec
    lists no required parameters for it, so a parameterless firing is within
    spec. Standard events are the event type Meta supports "for logging
    conversions, optimizing for conversions, and building audiences."
    Source: "Specifications for Meta Pixel standard events"
    https://www.facebook.com/business/help/402791146561655
    One implementation flag, stated neutrally: Meta's own note in that same
    spec says the PageView event is included as part of the standard Pixel
    base code. So IF the completion page loads the standard base snippet
    unmodified, it will also send a PageView from that page — which differs
    from your design statement of "no PageView." Whether your build includes
    or omits that call is a fact your planned test can confirm. [SOURCED
    about the base code; UNKNOWN for your build]

(b) WILL THE SCRIPT PICK UP THE fbclid, SET COOKIES, LINK EVENT TO CLICK?
    The mechanism is Meta's documented behavior. [SOURCED]
    Meta for Developers, "ClickID and the fbp and fbc Parameters" (updated
    9 Jan 2026):
    https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/conversions-api/parameters/fbp-and-fbc
    What it documents:
      - The ClickID (fbclid) is "passed with the URL of an advertiser's
        website when a user clicks an ad on Facebook and/or Instagram."
      - "Meta Pixel is installed on the website. In this case, Meta Pixel
        automatically stores ClickID value in the _fbc browser cookie once
        available."
      - "When the Meta Pixel is installed on a website, and the Pixel uses
        first-party cookies, the Pixel automatically saves a unique
        identifier to an _fbp cookie for the website domain if one does not
        already exist."
      - The stored value's format is fb.<subdomainIndex>.<creationTime>.
        <fbclid>, and the ClickID is case-sensitive — do not modify it.
    Applied to your design: the Pixel does not need to have run on the
    landing page. It needs the fbclid to be present in the URL of the page
    where it DOES run, at load time. Your completion URL carries
    ?fbclid=<snapshot> in the query string, which is exactly the documented
    input. On that basis the design's pickup step rests on documented
    behavior, not hope. Conditions and failure modes to keep in the plan:
      - The fbclid must survive untransformed into the completion URL
        (case-sensitive; no lowercasing/re-encoding anywhere in your chain,
        and no redirect that drops the query string before the Pixel loads).
      - Upstream stripping exists in the wild (for example iOS Link Tracking
        Protection in some contexts can remove fbclid from shared/clicked
        links). Your landing-page first-party capture is the mitigation,
        but the capture itself depends on the fbclid surviving the
        click-to-landing hop. [General ecosystem fact; rate for your
        traffic UNKNOWN]
      - The _fbp created on the completion page is fresh (no prior history
        on that subdomain) and there is no advanced matching, so event match
        quality rests almost entirely on fbc. That is enough for click
        attribution; it is weaker for cross-device/people matching.
    Whether YOUR build actually picks it up remains [UNKNOWN] until your
    planned test (plan line 241) runs — that test is the right one, and as
    your plan itself says, it proves carriage, not credit.

(c) ATTRIBUTION AND DELIVERY.  Mechanism sourced; results not guaranteed.
    With fbc attached, Meta has the join key it documents for tying the
    event to the specific ad click, inside the campaign's attribution
    setting (default for Sales is 7-day click). Meta's "Registrations
    completed" metric "counts Complete Registration events that are tracked
    by one of your Meta Business Tools... and attributed to your ads," with
    statistical modeling used in some cases where events can't be counted
    directly:
    https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1764313906951681
    [SOURCED] Prerequisites on our side are already met: the dataset is
    connected to the operative ad account (confirmed 9 Nov).
    Delivery is the genuinely weak leg, and your plan already prices this
    in ("firing and delivery aren't guaranteed"): a single-try browser fire
    can be lost to ad blockers, a closed tab, a failed load, or consent
    timing. Meta can only attribute events it receives. No source can
    quantify your loss rate in advance. [UNKNOWN]

(d) OPTIMIZATION, LEARNING, VOLUME.  Threshold sourced; reachability unknown.
    Once Meta receives CompleteRegistration and it shows active in Events
    Manager, it can be selected as the optimization event for a Sales
    (website) ad set — that is the standard mechanics of standard events
    [SOURCED, same spec as (a)]. The learning bar is about 50 optimization
    events per ad set in the week after the last significant edit; below
    that, expect "Learning Limited":
    https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700
    https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981
    You are right that this states Meta's preference, not your outcome: we
    have no cost-per-click or cost-per-sign-up data for this product, so
    whether $625 buys 50 sign-ups in 7 days is [UNKNOWN], not "can't." For
    scale only — arithmetic, not a forecast [SPECULATION, labeled]: at a
    hypothetical $1 per click, $625 buys ~625 clicks and 50 sign-ups needs
    an 8% click-to-sign-up rate; at $2 per click it needs 16%. Whether
    either is realistic for Numista.Ai is exactly what a first campaign
    would measure. Also note Learning Limited is not a block: the ad set
    still delivers and spends; optimization is just weaker.
    REVISED VERDICT on Path 4: with fbclid forwarded, the design is
    technically coherent — valid event, documented pickup, attributable if

ASK 3 — THE THREE CLAIMS, SOURCED AND LABELED
---------------------------------------------
CLAIM 1: "Website sign-ups should use Sales rather than Leads."
  LABEL: RECOMMENDATION grounded in Meta's official objective definitions
  (not a hard requirement — Meta documents what each objective is FOR;
  nothing physically stops a Leads campaign, but website registration
  optimization is a Sales/website-conversions pattern).
    - Sales: "for campaigns where you want to find people likely to
      purchase your product or service... maximize sales or conversions on
      your website," and it "can also optimize... for other actions" —
      conversions on your website are its documented home:
      https://www.facebook.com/business/help/672411877425804
    - Leads: campaigns "to find potential customers... gather information
      from new leads" via Instant Forms (its exclusive feature), calls, or
      messages — i.e., lead capture, not completed account registrations
      on your own website:
      https://www.facebook.com/business/help/387294348401675
    Precision note: "Lead" is also a standard EVENT (a form submission or
    trial sign-up, per the standard-events spec). If Eric ever optimizes a
    website flow inside Sales, the event choice CompleteRegistration vs
    Lead is a modeling choice; the OBJECTIVE point stands either way.

CLAIM 2: "Landing page views are reported without a Pixel."
  LABEL: SOURCED (Meta metric definition), with a scope limit.
    "Landing page views are tracked when people use the in-app browser on
    Meta technologies to visit your destination web pages or by having a
    Meta Pixel installed on them." Scope limit: without a Pixel, only
    in-app-browser loads are counted (plus modeling) — a subset of all
    landing loads, so treat LPVs as indicative, not exact.
    https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361750134220832

CLAIM 3: "The AEM / prioritized-events configuration is gone."
  LABEL: SOURCED (requirement removed by Meta).
    "About Meta's Aggregated Event Measurement": "You no longer need to
    prioritize 8 conversion events per domain for web conversion
    optimization... The Aggregated Event Measurement tab in Meta Events
    Manager has been removed... You aren't required to verify your website
    domains for purposes related to event configuration... You don't need
    to select a conversion domain when you create a campaign in Ads
    Manager."
    https://www.facebook.com/business/help/721422165168355
