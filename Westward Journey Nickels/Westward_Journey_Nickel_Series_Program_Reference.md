# Westward Journey Nickel Series™ (2004–2005) — Official Program Reference

**Authorizing Statute:** *American 5-Cent Coin Design Continuity Act of 2003* (Public Law 108-15; 117 Stat. 615; 31 U.S.C. § 5112(p)).  
**Authority & Issuer:** United States Mint, Department of the Treasury (`usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/circulating-coins/nickel/westward-journey-nickels`).

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Program Status

### Does it have its own official U.S. Coin Program?
**YES, absolutely.** The **Westward Journey Nickel Series™** is a distinct, Congressionally authorized multi-year circulating commemorative coin program. 

Just like the **50 State Quarters** (P.L. 105-124), **America the Beautiful Quarters** (P.L. 110-456), **American Women Quarters** (P.L. 116-330), and **Lincoln Bicentennial Cents** (P.L. 109-145), the Westward Journey Nickel Series is an independent statutory program designed to commemorate the bicentennials of the **Louisiana Purchase (1803)** and the **Lewis and Clark Expedition (1804–1806)**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Checklist Recommendation for Numista.AI:**  
> The Westward Journey Nickels (2004–2005) should **NOT** be buried in the standard 1938–2003 Jefferson Nickel checklist. They should have their own dedicated **"Westward Journey Nickel Series (2004–2005)"** program checklist (containing the 4 distinct reverse designs and unique 2005 cursive-script obverse across P, D, S Proof, and Satin Finish strikes).

## 2. Complete Design Timeline (2004–2006)

Across the two-year program (plus the 2006 redesign), the Mint produced **4 distinct reverse designs** and **2 new obverses**:

```
2004 (Obverse: 1938 Classic Felix Schlag Portrait)
  ├─ Reverse 1: Peace Medal (Louisiana Purchase)
  └─ Reverse 2: Keelboat (Lewis & Clark Missouri River)

2005 (Obverse: ONE-YEAR-ONLY Close-Up Portrait + Handwritten "Liberty" Script)
  ├─ Reverse 1: American Bison / Buffalo
  └─ Reverse 2: Ocean in View! ("Ocean in view! O! The joy!")

2006 "Return to Monticello" (Permanent Redesign)
  ├─ Obverse: New Forward-Facing Thomas Jefferson Portrait (by Jamie Franki)
  └─ Reverse: Classic Monticello Restored (Sculpted by John Mercanti)
```

## 3. The 4 Official Program Coins in Detail

### 🪙 1. 2004 Peace Medal Nickel (Spring 2004)
* **Theme:** Bicentennial of the Louisiana Purchase (1803).
* **Obverse:** Classic left-facing portrait of Thomas Jefferson by Felix Schlag (used since 1938).
* **Reverse Design:** An adaptation of the original 1801 Jefferson Peace Medal presented to Native American leaders during the expedition. Features two clasped hands with a peace pipe (calumet) and tomahawk crossed above, with the inscription *"LOUISIANA PURCHASE"*, *"1803"*, and *"PEACE AND FRIENDSHIP"*.
* **Designer / Sculptor:** Norman E. Nemeth.
* **Official Mintages:**
  * **2004-P (Philadelphia):** 361,440,000
  * **2004-D (Denver):** 372,000,000
  * **2004-S (San Francisco Clad Proof):** 2,965,422

---

### 🪙 2. 2004 Keelboat Nickel (Fall 2004)
* **Theme:** Lewis and Clark Expedition navigating the Missouri River.
* **Obverse:** Classic left-facing portrait of Thomas Jefferson by Felix Schlag.
* **Reverse Design:** Depicts the 55-foot custom keelboat engineered by Captain Meriwether Lewis that transported the Corps of Discovery through the rivers of the Louisiana Territory, under full sail with Lewis and Clark in full uniform on the bow.
* **Designer / Sculptor:** Al Maletsky.
* **Official Mintages:**
  * **2004-P (Philadelphia):** 366,720,000
  * **2004-D (Denver):** 344,880,000
  * **2004-S (San Francisco Clad Proof):** 2,965,422

---

### 🪙 3. 2005 American Bison / Buffalo Nickel (Spring 2005)
* **Theme:** The western wildlife and Plains culture encountered by the expedition.
* **Obverse (Historic First!):** A brand new, close-up side profile of President Jefferson designed by Joe Fitzgerald and sculpted by Don Everhart, based on the 1789 bust by Jean-Antoine Houdon. Features the word **"Liberty"** in Jefferson’s actual handwritten script from his draft of the Declaration of Independence. *(First obverse design change on the nickel in 67 years!).*
* **Reverse Design:** A majestic American Bison standing on a grassy rise, paying tribute to the historical significance of the buffalo and reminiscent of James Earle Fraser’s famous 1913 Buffalo Nickel.
* **Designer / Sculptor:** Designed by Jamie Franki; sculpted by Donna Weaver.
* **Official Mintages:**
  * **2005-P (Philadelphia):** 448,320,000
  * **2005-D (Denver):** 485,760,000
  * **2005-S (San Francisco Clad Proof):** 3,344,679
  * **2005-P Satin Finish (Mint Set):** 1,160,000
  * **2005-D Satin Finish (Mint Set):** 1,160,000
  * *Special Variety:* **2005-D "Speared Bison" Mint Error** (popular high-value collector variety caused by a deep die gouge through the buffalo's torso).

---

### 🪙 4. 2005 "Ocean in View!" Nickel (Fall 2005)
* **Theme:** The triumphant arrival of the Corps of Discovery at the Pacific Ocean.
* **Obverse:** 2005 one-year-only close-up Jefferson portrait with handwritten "Liberty" script.
* **Reverse Design:** Depicts a rugged view of the Pacific Ocean shoreline (based on a photograph by Andrew E. Cier), with the famous quote inscribed in Captain William Clark’s distinct journal entry spelling:  
  **`"Ocean in view! O! The joy!"`** *(November 7, 1805)*.
* **Designer / Sculptor:** Designed by Joe Fitzgerald; sculpted by Donna Weaver.
* **Official Mintages:**
  * **2005-P (Philadelphia):** 394,080,000
  * **2005-D (Denver):** 411,120,000
  * **2005-S (San Francisco Clad Proof):** 3,344,679
  * **2005-P Satin Finish (Mint Set):** 1,160,000
  * **2005-D Satin Finish (Mint Set):** 1,160,000

---

## 4. Why You Found 3 Different Reverses in Pocket Change

If you are sorting through 2004–2006 nickels, you will encounter **3 main modern reverse designs**:
1. **The American Bison / Buffalo** (2005)
2. **"Ocean in View! O! The joy!"** (2005)
3. **The Restored Monticello** (2006–Present) *(or the 2004 Peace Medal / Keelboat)*.

In 2006, Congress mandated under P.L. 108-15 that the nickel return permanently to Monticello on the reverse, while introducing a new forward-facing Jefferson obverse (which remains our circulating nickel design today).
