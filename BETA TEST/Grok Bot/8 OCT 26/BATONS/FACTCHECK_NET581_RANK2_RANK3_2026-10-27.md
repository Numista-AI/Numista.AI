### Fix C — Remove Grand Watermelon from Gold Certificates (**priority**)
**OLD:**  
`Gold Certificates: … 1882/1905 $20 "Technicolor", 1890/1891 $1,000 "Grand Watermelon", Series 1934 $100,000 Woodrow Wilson …`

**NEW:**  
`Gold Certificates: … Series 1905 $20 “Technicolor”; Series 1934 $100,000 Woodrow Wilson (Federal Reserve / Treasury gold-transfer use only; not public circulation).`  
`[Separate / related large-size:] Series 1890 $1,000 Treasury Note (“Grand Watermelon”) — Coin Note, not a Gold Certificate.`

### Fix D — Move Porthole out of Legal Tender (**priority**)
**OLD:**  
`Legal Tender Notes (1862): … 1901 $10 "Bison", 1923 Porthole $5.`

**NEW:**  
`Legal Tender / United States Notes (1862+): … 1901 $10 “Bison” Note.`  
`[Under Silver Certificates:] Series 1923 $5 “Porthole” (Lincoln in circular frame) — Silver Certificate, not Legal Tender.`

### Fix E — Hibernia circulation
**OLD:**  
`Struck for Ireland by William Wood, rejected in Ireland and exported in large quantities to the American colonies where they circulated widely.`

**NEW:**  
`Struck for Ireland by William Wood (1722–1724); strongly opposed in Ireland. Some examples reached British America through trade; substantial “wide colonial circulation” is not well documented — do not overclaim.`

### Fix F — 1767 sou / Louisiana
**OLD:**  
`Struck for French colonies; widely counterstamped RF (République Française) in 1793. Circulated widely in French Louisiana and Mississippi Valley.`

**NEW:**  
`Paris Mint 1767 copper sol (12 deniers) for French colonial use; many later counterstamped RF (République Française), 1793, associated with Guadeloupe / Collot redistribution. Catalogued in U.S. colonial type sets by Red Book tradition; do not claim official or wide circulation in Louisiana (not French in 1767/1793).`

### Fix G — Spanish LT chronology
**OLD:**  
`Authorized as legal tender in the US by the Act of February 9, 1793 (and confirmed by the Coinage Act of 1792 adopting the Spanish milled dollar as the basis of the US dollar unit). Remained legal tender until revoked on February 21, 1857.`

**NEW:**  
`Coinage Act of April 2, 1792 defined the U.S. dollar by the Spanish milled dollar then current. Act of February 9, 1793 made Spanish milled dollars (and parts) legal tender at specified weights/rates. Act of February 21, 1857 repealed prior foreign-coin legal-tender statutes.`

### Fix H — NJ / MA significance (tighten, still Supported)
**OLD (NJ):** `First appearance of the national motto E PLURIBUS UNUM on an American coin.`  
**NEW (NJ):** `First appearance of E PLURIBUS UNUM on an American **coin** (New Jersey state copper, 1786+).`

**OLD (MA):** `First American state coinage with explicit denominated values (CENT / HALF CENT) on the design.`  
**NEW (MA):** `First Confederation-era / U.S. state coins to bear the on-design denominations **CENT** and **HALF CENT**.`

### Fix I — CSA cent provenance nuance
**OLD:**  
`Engraved by Robert Lovett Jr. of Philadelphia. … Census: Exactly 12-16 original specimens in copper-nickel struck by Lovett.`

**NEW:**  
`Dies/engraving by Robert Lovett Jr. (Philadelphia). Traditional census **~12–16** original copper-nickel pieces (classic figure 12; estimates vary — mark Estimated, not Mint).
