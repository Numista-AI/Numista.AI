## 1. Rank 2: Colonial & Confederation Empires (C4–C7)

### C4: State Coppers (1785–1788)
*Authorized state-level token/copper currencies issued under the Articles of Confederation prior to the federal Constitution.*
1. **Connecticut Coppers (1785–1788):**
   - **Mints:** Company for Coining Coppers (New Haven), Company of Coiners.
   - **Obverse:** Draped or armored bust facing left or right, legend AUCTORI CONNEC.
   - **Reverse:** Seated Goddess of Liberty facing left holding branch and staff, legend INDE ET LIB (Independence and Liberty).
   - **Standard:** Nominally 144 grains copper. Hundreds of die varieties (Miller classifications).
2. **New Jersey Coppers (1786–1788):**
   - **Mints:** Rahway, Morristown, Elizabethtown (Walter Mould, Albion Cox, Thomas Goadsby).
   - **Obverse:** Horse's head above a plow, legend NOVA CAESAREA with date below.
   - **Reverse:** United States heraldic shield with 13 vertical stripes, legend E PLURIBUS UNUM.
   - **Significance:** First appearance of the national motto E PLURIBUS UNUM on an American coin. Maris variety classifications.
3. **Massachusetts Coppers (1787–1788):**
   - **Mints:** Boston (Commonwealth of Massachusetts mint at Boston Neck).
   - **Denominations:** Half Cent (1787–1788) and Cent (1787–1788).
   - **Obverse:** Standing Native American holding bow and arrow, single star in field, legend COMMONWEALTH.
   - **Reverse:** American heraldic eagle with displayed wings, shield on breast inscribed CENT or HALF CENT, legend MASSACHUSETTS and date.
   - **Significance:** First American state coinage with explicit denominated values (CENT / HALF CENT) on the design.
4. **Vermont Coppers (1785–1788):**
   - **Mints:** Rupert, VT (Reuben Harmon Jr.).
   - **Types:**
     - *Landscape Type (1785–1786):* Sun rising over green mountains with plow in foreground, legend VERMONTS RES PUBLICA / VERMONTIS RES PUBLICA; reverse eye of Providence with 13 rays and 13 stars, legend STELLA QUARTA DECIMA (The 14th Star).
     - *Bust Type (1786–1788):* Armored/draped bust, legend AUCTORI VERMON; reverse seated Liberty, INDE ET LIB.

### C5: British Colonial Circulating in America
1. **Virginia Halfpenny (1773):**
   - Struck at the Royal Mint (Tower of London) for colony of Virginia.
   - Obverse: Bust of George III right, GEORGIUS III REX.
   - Reverse: Virginia colonial coat of arms, crowned escutcheon, legend VIRGINIA and date 1773. Only authorized English colonial issue for a mainland colony.
2. **Rosa Americana Series (1722–1724):**
   - Patentee William Wood (Bath metal / composition of brass, zinc, silver wash). Twopence, Penny, Halfpenny. Crowned or uncrowned rose device, legend ROSA AMERICANA UTILE DULCI.
3. **Hibernia Coppers (1722–1724):**
   - Struck for Ireland by William Wood, rejected in Ireland and exported in large quantities to the American colonies where they circulated widely.

### C6: French Colonial Issues (Louisiana & French America)
1. **1721–1722 Copper 9 Deniers (Compagnie des Indes):**
   - Struck at La Rochelle and Rouen for the French colonies in Louisiana and Canada.
   - Device: Crossed royal L cyphers under crown; reverse legend COLONIES FRANCOISES.
2. **1767 Sou / 12 Deniers (Paris Mint):**
   - Struck for French colonies; widely counterstamped RF (République Française) in 1793. Circulated widely in French Louisiana and Mississippi Valley.

### C7: Spanish Colonial Silver (8 Reales Money of Account)
- **Legal Basis:** Authorized as legal tender in the US by the Act of February 9, 1793 (and confirmed by the Coinage Act of 1792 adopting the Spanish milled dollar as the basis of the US dollar unit). Remained legal tender until revoked on February 21, 1857.
- **Type Distinctions:**
  1. *Pillar Dollar (1732–1772):* Columns of Hercules flanking crowned twin globes (VTRAQUE VNUM), struck at Mexico City, Lima, Potosí, Guatemala.
  2. *Portrait Dollar (1772–1825):* Laureate bust of Charles III, Charles IV, or Ferdinand VII; reverse crowned Spanish arms between Pillars of Hercules (PLUS ULTRA).

## 2. Rank 3: Confederate Coinage & Early Federal Paper (H2–H3, N1–N7)

### H2: Confederate States of America Coinage (1861)
1. **1861 Confederate Half Dollar:**
   - **Authority:** Struck at the former US Mint at New Orleans under Confederate authority (Superintendent William A. Elmore).
   - **Obverse:** Standard US 1861 Seated Liberty die.
   - **Reverse:** CSA heraldic shield with 7 stars (seceded states) surmounted by Liberty cap, surrounded by wreath of cotton and sugar cane, legend CONFEDERATE STATES OF AMERICA and HALF DOL.
   - **Census:** Exactly **4 original specimens** struck on a hand screw press.
   - **Restrikes:** In 1879, J.W. Scott planed the reverses of 500 genuine 1861 US half dollars and re-struck them with the original CSA reverse die; also struck 500 white metal tokens.
2. **1861 Confederate Cent:**
   - Engraved by Robert Lovett Jr. of Philadelphia. Obverse Minerva in French Liberty cap; reverse cotton and tobacco wreath enclosing 1 CENT.
   - Census: Exactly **12-16 original specimens** in copper-nickel struck by Lovett. Later restrikes by Capt. John W. Haseltine (1874) and Robert Bashlow (1961 transfer dies).

### H3: Confederate States Currency (1861–1864)
- **Series:** Types 1–72 categorized by Criswell and Fricke systems.
- **Issuing Centers:** Montgomery (first capital, 1861 — rare Type 1–4 issues: $50, $100, $500, $1,000), Richmond (1861–1864 subsequent issues).
- **Themes/Imagery:** Southern agricultural scenes (slaves in cotton fields), historical statesmen (Washington, Jefferson Davis, Stonewall Jackson, Judah P. Benjamin), allegorical representations (Commerce, Sailor, Goddess of Liberty).

### N1–N7: Early Federal & United States Paper Currency Types
1. **N1: Continental Currency (1775–1779):**
   - Authorized by the Continental Congress to fund the American Revolutionary War.
   - Emblems designed by Benjamin Franklin with Latin mottoes (e.g., SUSTINE VEL ABSTINE, MIND YOUR BUSINESS). Printed by Hall & Sellers, Philadelphia, with anti-counterfeiting nature-print leaves on reverse.
2. **N2: Demand Notes (1861) & Legal Tender / United States Notes (1862+):**
   - **Demand Notes (1861):** First federal paper currency; payable on demand in gold coin at designated sub-treasuries. Printed by American Bank Note Co. Known as original "Greenbacks" due to green security ink on reverse.
   - **Legal Tender Notes (1862):** Fiat currency backed by government credit (Act of Feb 25, 1862). Types include 1862 First Obligation, 1869 "Rainbow" Notes, 1880 Large Size, 1901 $10 "Bison", 1923 Porthole $5.
3. **N3: Silver Certificates (1878–1957) & Gold Certificates (1863–1934):**
   - **Silver Certificates:** Series 1886 Martha Washington $1, Series 1896 "Educational Series" ($1 History Instructing Youth, $2 Science Presenting Steam, $5 Electricity Presenting Light), Series 1899 Chief Black Hawk $5.
   - **Gold Certificates:** Backed by gold coin/bullion on deposit. 1882/1905 $20 "Technicolor", 1890/1891 $1,000 "Grand Watermelon", Series 1934 $100,000 Woodrow Wilson (internal Federal Reserve gold bullion transfers only).
4. **N6: Fractional Currency (1862–1876):**
   - Issued to alleviate extreme hoarding of silver/copper coins during the Civil War.
   - Five distinct issues: 1st Issue (Postage Currency with stamps), 2nd Issue (Washington in bronze oval), 3rd Issue (Spencer Clark $0.05, Fessenden, Spinner), 4th & 5th Issues (large seals, watermarked paper). Denominations: 3¢, 5¢, 10¢, 15¢, 25¢, 50¢.
5. **N7: Emergency WWII Overprint Notes (1942–1944):**
   - **Hawaii Overprints (1942–1944):** Brown Treasury seal with large bold HAWAII printed across reverse and on vertical obverse sides. In case of Japanese capture of Hawaiian Islands, currency would be declared worthless.
   - **North Africa / Europe Overprints (1942–1944):** Yellow Treasury seal on Silver Certificates for Operation Torch and Mediterranean Theater to prevent use if captured by Axis forces.
